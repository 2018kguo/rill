# 10 days in Japan: Tokyo, Hakone, Kyoto and Osaka

This plan assumes a first visit in spring or autumn, a moderate budget, and that you like walking. It flies into Tokyo and out of Osaka (Kansai), so you never backtrack. Prices are rough per-person estimates in yen; check current fares before you book.

## The route at a glance

| Day | Base | Highlights | Getting there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tokyo | Arrive, Shinjuku at night<br>Omoide Yokocho for dinner | Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner from Narita; Keikyu or monorail from Haneda |
| 2 | Tokyo | Meiji Shrine, Harajuku, Shibuya Crossing<br>Shibuya Sky at sunset | Metro |
| 3 | Tokyo | Asakusa and Senso-ji early<br>Ueno Park, Akihabara | Metro |
| 4 | Tokyo | Day trip to Nikko *or* Kamakura<br>(pick one) | JR or Tobu lines |
| 5 | Hakone | Lake Ashi cruise, ropeway, Owakudani<br>Stay in a ryokan with an onsen | Odakyu Romancecar from Shinjuku |
| 6 | Kyoto | Fushimi Inari (go before 8 am)<br>Gion in the evening | Shinkansen from Odawara |
| 7 | Kyoto | Arashiyama bamboo grove, Tenryu-ji<br>Kinkaku-ji in the afternoon | JR Sagano line, bus |
| 8 | Kyoto | Nara day trip: Todai-ji and the deer<br>Back for dinner in Pontocho | JR Nara line, about 45 min |
| 9 | Osaka | Osaka Castle, Dotonbori street food<br>Kuromon Market | JR Special Rapid, about 30 min |
| 10 | Osaka | Last-minute shopping, fly home | Haruka express to Kansai Airport |

## Before you go

- [x] Passport valid for the whole stay
- [x] Travel insurance
- [ ] Book the ryokan early: the good ones fill up months ahead in cherry blossom season
- [ ] Load a transit card (Suica or PASMO) on your phone
- [ ] Download offline maps for each city
- [ ] Carry some cash: smaller restaurants and temples may not take cards

## Tips that make the trip easier

1. **Use luggage forwarding.** Hotels can send your suitcase ahead to the next city for a small fee (*takkyubin*), so you can travel to Hakone with just a day bag.
2. **Start early at the famous spots.** Fushimi Inari, the Arashiyama bamboo grove and Senso-ji are calm at 7 am and packed by 10.
3. **Eat where the office workers eat.** Lunch sets (*teishoku*) at small restaurants are some of the best value meals in the country.
4. **Mind the etiquette.** Don't eat while walking in busy areas, keep your voice down on trains, and wash before getting into an onsen.

> If you only add one thing, make it a night in a ryokan. Dinner in your room, a hot spring bath and sleeping on futons is the part of the trip people remember most.

For planning, the official [Japan National Tourism Organization](https://www.japan.travel/en/) site and the [JR Pass calculator](https://www.jrpass.com/) are good starting points.
