## A few patterns worth noticing

- **Noble gases** (He, Ne, Ar, Kr) sit at the end of each period and are all gases at standard temperature and pressure.
- **Bromine** is the only element in this range that is a liquid at STP; mercury (80) is the other liquid element, outside this table.
- **Chromium** (3d<sup>5</sup> 4s<sup>1</sup>) and **copper** (3d<sup>10</sup> 4s<sup>1</sup>) break the simple filling order: a half-filled or filled d subshell is lower in energy, so one 4s electron moves into 3d. Niobium, molybdenum, ruthenium, rhodium, palladium and silver do the same in period 5.
- **Technetium** (43) is the lightest element with no stable isotopes, which is why its weight is shown as a mass number.
- Atomic weight rises with atomic number almost everywhere, but **argon (39.95) is heavier than potassium (39.098)**, one of the classic inversions that Mendeleev's ordering by properties got right.

> The categories follow the common periodic-table convention; some sources classify hydrogen, the metalloids and the post-transition metals slightly differently.
